# The five cases

Five real runs, between August and October 2026, described without the
projects' names or data. The numbers are referenced in [method.md](method.md).

## Case 1 · Designing a panel of agents (August)

**The question:** to have a decision critiqued by several agents, should you
vary the models or vary the angles?
**What came out:** orthogonal angles rather than opposite ones. It was not a
design test, but it is where the mandate rule was born.

## Case 2 · A cycling app, "which way do I ride" (September)

**The need:** a cyclist, at home, before heading out, wants to know which way
to ride given the wind and the weather. Three seconds and a thumb.
**Round 1:** three models, one brief that named the artefact (a compass rose)
and cited inspirations. Three times the same screen: dark navigation
instrument, signage typeface. The user's verdict: too "made by an AI".
**Round 2:** one model, the brief stripped of its artefacts, four mandates
(Swiss editorial, mainstream sports, Japanese, rupture). Four really
different proposals.
**Outcome:** a direction composed from three variants, with, for each, what
we refused to take from it. Three variants out of four had, unasked, made the
disagreement between weather models their finest element.

## Case 3 · A brand showreel (October)

**The need:** a short film presenting a consulting practice.
**The test:** Sonnet, blind and from scratch, against a fourth version by Opus
that had received all the feedback.
**Outcome:** Sonnet delivered clean work on the first attempt and the best
visual idea, but missed on judgement (reading rule, signature line). Opus did
the merge. The test was not neutral: the two did not start from the same
point. Measured cost: about 12 minutes and 185,000 tokens per variant.

## Case 4 · A teenager's home screen in a home-automation app (October)

**The need:** a high-school student opens the app on their phone in the
evening and in the morning. They want to know whether their alarm is set and
what they still have to do. It works if they open the page on their own and
do not experience it as a parental control panel.
**Mandates:** streetwear zine, kitchen brigade, the house talks to you, the
turning hour, rupture "a single sentence".
**Attractor:** the five defaults describe the same page (dark background, big
alarm card, grid of rounded tiles, icons, badges). No variant kept it.
**Outcome:** the rupture variant, as it was. The composition of two variants
was rejected because it put several blocks back on the first screen. All five
variants reordered the screen according to the time of day, though four of
them had not received that as their mandate.

## Case 5 · The idle screen of a wall tablet (October)

**The need:** a tablet on the living-room wall. People walk past two to four
metres away, without stopping, in dim light. In under two seconds, they know
whether something needs doing or whether all is well.
**Mandates:** eight this time (station signage, newspaper front page, light
and matter, empty space, dashboard instrument, Bauhaus poster, note on the
fridge, rupture tear-off calendar), plus the mockup that existed before the
divergence, kept as variant 0.
**Attractor:** eight defaults out of eight identical in substance (navy, one
warm accent, row of cards, geometric grotesque). No variant kept it.
**Outcome:** the user kept their variant 0 as it was. Opus's composition,
built on the skeleton of variant 0, was judged to bring no new idea. All
eight variants handed the state of the house to a single object that changes
nature (a dot, a needle, a flat colour, a label), without the brief asking
for it.
