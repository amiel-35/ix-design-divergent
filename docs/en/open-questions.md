# What we do not know yet

## One run per cell

Each brief and mandate combination ran only once. The convergence observed in
case 2 may be partly due to chance.

## Two variables changed at once

Between the two rounds of case 2, the brief lost its artefacts and each
variant got its own mandate. We do not know which of the two changes mattered
most. Settling it would need a control round: same brief, only the mandate
varies.

## Sonnet everywhere

Sonnet produces the variants, and since case 5 it also does the composition,
which Opus did before. The choice rests on the author's experience, on a
single test (case 3) that was not neutral, and on the Opus composition of
case 5 judged to have "created nothing". Finding 1 says the model weighs
little against the brief. The two can be reconciled (the brief gives the
variance, the model gives the quality of the idea), but this has not been
measured.

## Is the composition worth it?

In case 2, the chosen direction was a composition. In cases 4 and 5, the
composition was rejected and a single piece was kept as it was:
- in case 4, the composition put back density that the best variant had
  precisely removed;
- in case 5, it assembled rules taken from the variants onto an existing
  skeleton, with no new idea. That is exactly what the composition step asks for, and it is
  what it was blamed for.

Decided after case 5: the composition is now an option, offered after the
user has seen the variants, and it runs on Sonnet. Hypothesis still to test:
when a variant's stance is economy (one sentence, almost nothing), assembling
destroys it by construction. Composition would work better between rich
variants than with a minimalist rupture.

## Keeping the existing version as variant 0

In case 5, the pre-divergence mockup was shown next to the variants, and it
won. That partly contradicts the rule "no variant sees the existing version":
variant 0 contaminated no variant, but it was in the final comparison. We do
not know whether it is a bias (we prefer what we already made) or a good
practice (divergence also serves to confirm a choice).
